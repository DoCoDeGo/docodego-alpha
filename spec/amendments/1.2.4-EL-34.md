# Amendment EL-34 → 1.2.4

*An amendment scaffold for The launch gate (laws/34-the-launch-gate.md). It changes nothing
by itself: the law file, the version stamp, and the log move only when
this amendment is signed and accepted.*

## What changed

The founder-signed paragraph's "who" parenthetical: `who (a legal
identifier or handle, recorded verbatim)` becomes:

> who (a typed identifier or handle, recorded verbatim; an identifier
> the engine derives from the environment is recorded as a versioned
> fingerprint of it, never as it stands)

One sentence is added to the honest-limit paragraph:

> A fingerprinted identifier is privacy, not anonymity: it resists a
> stranger's lookup, not a determined guesser who already suspects the
> environment.

No severity change, no duty change; the tiers stay strict
(launch-passed first, one signature per act) and the attestation
sentence stands untouched. Descriptive, not normative: the law's
conformance paragraph may name the mechanism it now enforces (a typed
identifier verbatim; a derived environment identifier as
`fp:v1:<hex>`, salted per record).

## Why

The founder's second ruling of 2026-11-08 (recorded verbatim in the
record's cycles log, unit 480): the identifier may default to a
system fingerprint, the typed name stays optional — `--system` /
`--name "some name"` — and the concern is adoption: "individuals
won't adopt this if they suspect their privacy is at risk." The law's
"recorded verbatim", read over a derived environment identifier,
would force the record to publish machine noise that names a person's
machine (`code@admins-MBP.nbn` is the class of string at issue). The
wording now names the consent split the machine enforces: a typed
identifier is verbatim (it is the attestation — a human typed it,
that is the consent); a derived identifier is fingerprinted (it is
the witness — the same fingerprint in one record means the same
environment, and no reader learns which machine). The engine lands
the split in the same unit (payload v25/v26): scrypt fingerprints,
salted per record at `04-govern/signature-salt`, committed so the
record stays self-contained; `--by` remains a documented alias of
`--name`; `--yes` and the one-signature rails are byte-for-byte
untouched. Existing rows stand as recorded — append-only; the scheme
protects rows from the day it ships.

## What risk it answers

The adoption risk: a record that publishes environment identities
suspected of being personally linkable scares off the very signers
the accountability tier needs. And the honesty risk in the other
direction: a fingerprint claimed as anonymity would be the record
claiming more than its mechanism delivers — the added sentence prices
the protection exactly (resists lookup, not a determined guesser), so
the record never over-claims. The cross-record correlation risk is
answered structurally: every record's salt differs, so no global
handle exists.
