# Amendment EL-34 → 1.2.3

*An amendment scaffold for The launch gate (laws/34-the-launch-gate.md). It changes nothing
by itself: the law file, the version stamp, and the log move only when
this amendment is signed and accepted.*

## What changed

The law's "The law" section, founder-signed paragraph, one phrase:
`who (a legal name, recorded verbatim)` becomes `who (a legal identifier
or handle, recorded verbatim)`. No severity change, no duty change, no
other law's meaning changes; the tier stays strict (launch-passed first,
one signature per act) and the attestation limit sentence stands
untouched. The engine already runs the derived flow (unit 450: `sign`
derives the identifier from git or system details, or accepts a typed
`--by`; the whole act is shown and confirmed once) — this amendment
brings the law's words to what the machine does.

## Why

The founder's ruling (2026-11-08, recorded verbatim in the record's
cycles log, unit 450): the signature should not demand typed personal
details — the identifier derives, and the act is confirmed, not
transcribed. The law's "a legal name" named a ceremony the record never
had the power to perform: the honest limit (stated in this same law)
says the signature witnesses, it does not prove identity — so the
recorded string was never a legally verified name, only an identifier
or handle. The wording now says what is true. Its grill rode the
carrying unit's round (450's grill.md, AC5).

## What risk it answers

(EL-30) The law's words outliving the mechanism: a law that demanded a
typed legal name over a tool that derives the identifier would make the
law text a record of a ceremony that does not exist — the exact
drift `canon verify`'s command-vs-law check exists to catch. And the
residual reading risk: "legal name" invites a reader to think the
record holds a legally significant name, which the attestation limit
explicitly denies. The rewording closes both: the law names the
mechanism it enforces, and the string it records is called what it is.

## The words before

**founder-signed** — a named person attested the act. The signature is
an act receipt: who (a legal name, recorded verbatim), what role, when
(the local calendar date), over what evidence (the sha256 of the
unit's spec and delivered receipt at signing time). The tiers are
strict — no signature without launch-passed, no bypass flag — and one
signature per act: a second signature rewrites a record the ledger
keeps once, and is refused.
