> # Engine law 34 — The launch gate
>
> *Pillar: record — delivery ends the loop's own judgment; what happens
> after it needs gates that are not the loop grading its own homework.*
>
> ## The law
>
> A delivered unit is the loop's acceptance of its own work — it proves
> the contract held, not that the system should ship. Two tiers stand
> beyond it, and each is written by the gate that earned it, by nothing
> else:
>
> **launch-passed** — the unit's evidence survives a fresh audit of
> reality: the delivered receipt on file, every proof path still
> resolving (evidence that vanished since delivery proves nothing, and
> the vanished paths are named), the record's own check pass error-clean,
> and the delivery rows present in the tracker and the delivery log. The
> gate refuses on any failed check and records the attempt; the tier is
> never written over a refusal.
>
> **founder-signed** — a named person attested the act. The signature is
> an act receipt: who (a legal name, recorded verbatim), what role, when
> (the local calendar date), over what evidence (the sha256 of the
> unit's spec and delivered receipt at signing time). The tiers are
> strict — no signature without launch-passed, no bypass flag — and one
> signature per act: a second signature rewrites a record the ledger
> keeps once, and is refused.
>
> The signature's honest limit, stated wherever the record shows one: a
> signature is an attestation by command execution. It witnesses that
> the named person ran the signing command over the named evidence. It
> does not cryptographically prove the human's identity, and no output
> of the record may claim otherwise. What it buys is accountability: a
> person put their name on an act, and the act's evidence is hashed to
> what stood on disk when they did.
>
> ## Why
>
> The loop's gates guard the loop's own work; every one of them is the
> system grading its own homework. The commissions that matter — a
> production release, a boundary decision, a public claim — deserve a
> gate the loop cannot grant itself. The launch gate re-opens the
> evidence to reality (proofs still exist, the record still checks
> clean) instead of trusting the moment of delivery; the signature gate
> puts the act in a named person's hands instead of the agent's. And the
> honest limit is the point: a record that pretended its signatures were
> cryptographic would be manufacturing the very fiction this system
> exists to refuse.
>
> ## Violation
>
> A launch-passed unit whose evidence no longer resolves; a founder-signed
> unit with no signature receipt, or with a receipt whose evidence hashes
> no longer match the files they hashed; a status moved to either tier by
> hand; a bypass flag that signs over an unlaunched unit.
>
> ## Conformance
>
> Checked today: `docodego launch` runs the four checks over the
> delivered unit and refuses naming what failed; `docodego sign` requires
> launch-passed (refused otherwise, no bypass), appends one row per act
> to the signatures ledger, writes the unit's receipt with the evidence
> hashes, and refuses a second signature of the same act; frontmatter
> validation accepts the two tier statuses and nothing beyond them. The
> statuses ride EL-33's rails law: gate-written only, forward only.
> Direction: the audit sweep gains a launch-tier check (a launch-passed
> unit's proofs re-resolve on every sweep, not only at launch time) as a
> follow-up unit in the record's debt map.

## What changed

A new engine law is added at EL-34 (veto, record pillar), carrying the
post-delivery tiers with their enforceability named: which gate sets
which tier (launch → launch-passed, sign → founder-signed), what each
tier's gate checks, what refuses a bypass or a second signature today
(the commands refuse, evidence recorded), and the signature model's
attestation limit stated in the law text itself. This draft is
independent of its batch siblings (EL-18, EL-33): separate files,
separate sign-offs; whichever is accepted flips with the version bump,
the others stay draft.

## Why

Delivery is the loop accepting its own work — the strongest evidence the
loop can produce, and still self-graded. The commissions that matter
deserve a gate the loop cannot grant itself: reality re-audited (the
launch gate), and a named person accountable for the act (the signature
gate). The record also refuses to overstate what a CLI signature is: an
attestation, honestly bounded, with the boundary printed beside every
receipt.

## What risk it answers

Two risks. The quiet rot: evidence that existed at delivery silently
vanishing afterward while the unit stays "delivered" forever — the launch
gate re-checks reality instead of trusting a past moment. And signature
theater: a system that implies cryptographic authority it does not have
— answered by stating the attestation limit in the law, the ledger
header, and every receipt.

## Grilling (non-author, 2026-11-08)

Ten questions from a reader who did not write the draft; answers on the
record, revisions listed below.

- GQ-1 asked: Who can sign — is "founder" an identity the system
  verifies, or just a role string anyone can type?
  answered: a role string the record quotes verbatim — the law states the
  attestation limit; identity verification is out of scope and named as
  such. Revised: the law text and the receipt template both carry the
  limit sentence.
- GQ-2 asked: Why must signing require launch-passed — who does that
  gate serve, the signer or the record?
  answered: the record — the signature's evidence hashes include the
  launch state; a signature over an unlaunched unit would attest an act
  the record cannot reconstruct.
- GQ-3 asked: What stops an agent from signing with the founder's name?
  answered: nothing cryptographic — the receipt records who RAN the
  command (the environment's agent provenance lives in the tracker row),
  and the law says the record witnesses, not proves. The residual risk is
  named, not hidden.
- GQ-4 asked: Can a launch-passed unit's evidence rot after passing?
  answered: yes — the gate re-checks at launch time only; the follow-up
  (sweep-time re-check) is named in Conformance as a debt-map unit.
- GQ-5 asked: Why refuse a second signature instead of appending it?
  answered: one act, one receipt — appending would let two signatures of
  the same unit disagree about the same evidence; the law keeps the
  ledger append-only per act.
- GQ-6 asked: What if the founder wants to sign WITHOUT the launch audit
  — their record, their call?
  answered: the strict tiers are the design; a founder who wants to
  override reality has the record's own escape hatch (an intent/cycles
  entry recorded verbatim), not a silent flag.
- GQ-7 asked: Does launch-passed mean "ship it"?
  answered: no — it means the evidence still holds and the record is
  clean; shipping is the signer's act, which is exactly why the
  signature tier exists below it.
- GQ-8 asked: What do the tiers do to retirement — can a founder-signed
  unit retire?
  answered: yes — retirement moves the unit to its tomb and stamps it
  (EL-33's rule: retirement mints no status); the tiers ride along in the
  tomb copy.
- GQ-9 asked: Why hash spec.md and delivered.md and not the whole unit?
  answered: those two files ARE the act — the contract and the loop's
  acceptance of it. The whole-unit hash is the tomb's job (150-retire's
  read-back verified move), not the signature's.
- GQ-10 asked: Is launch-passed a quality judgment?
  answered: no — it is existence-and-coherence (evidence resolves, record
  clean). Quality was the score's job at delivery; the launch gate only
  asks whether that delivery still stands.

Revisions from the round: the attestation-limit sentence moved into the
law body and the receipt itself (was only in the amendment prose); the
launch-vs-shipping distinction (GQ-7) added to the law's why; the
retirement interaction (GQ-8) named in Conformance.
