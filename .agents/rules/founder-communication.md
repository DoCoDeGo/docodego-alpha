# The founder reads whole, and so does everyone else

Five laws for every founder-facing word and every product word:

1. **Correct context first.** Name the unit, its state, and the
   evidence before any claim. The founder never has to ask where a
   sentence sits or what it refers to.
2. **Plain English.** Short words over long ones; every acronym
   spelled out on first use ("decision record (ADR)") or not used at
   all. The founder is not the audience for shorthand invented in the
   room — an unexplained acronym sends the reader away to ask, and a
   question asked twice is work done twice.
3. **Concise.** The shortest honest form. Length hides meaning.
4. **Questions before work.** Every question asked is answered before
   implementation begins. A skipped question is a silent scope
   decision made by whoever skipped it.
5. **Meaningful at both ends.** The product's words read whole to a
   high school kid and to an industry veteran — every term introduced
   before it is used, and nothing dumbed down. The kid test: could a
   first-time reader follow without leaving the page? The veteran
   test: is every sentence earning its place, with no fluff padding
   the simplicity?

The failure this prevents: approval on a wrong understanding, and the
slow leak of clarification rounds that one clear first telling would
have saved.
