# The 36 Questions — Grounding Framework v3

*Vendored whole into this skill — the walk functions without any external source; this file is the questions' only home in a planted record. (Historical origin: a quick-reference edition of the Grounding framework.)*

State the goal first. Walk the layers in order. For each question, write a real answer. When a question doesn't pass, consider three things in order: the evaluation may be wrong, the question may not apply, the idea may be redesignable.

## Layer I — Reality
*Does the idea correspond to anything real that anyone needs?*

- 1.1 — Is the problem real, in concrete terms?
- 1.2 — Are the affected people findable?
- 1.3 — Does the problem matter enough for affected people to act on it?
- 1.4 — What do affected people currently use, and what is wrong with those alternatives?
- 1.5 — Have you watched at least three people use the alternatives?
- 1.6 — *(Conditional, non-commercial only)* Who benefits, how is benefit measured, and is the benefit-to-cost ratio favourable?

## Layer II — Timing
*Is now the right moment for this idea?*

- 2.1 — What has changed in the world recently that makes this idea more possible than it was five years ago?
- 2.2 — Is the change real or hype?
- 2.3 — Is the change durable, or could it reverse?
- 2.4 — Are you early, on time, or late?
- 2.5 — Why has nobody else done this yet, or done it well?
- 2.6 — Are too many other people doing this right now?

## Layer III — Viability
*Can the work sustain itself, given the maker's goal? All goal-conditional.*

- 3.1 — What does this idea need to keep running for one year, three years, ten years?
- 3.2 — Where do the resources come from, and how reliable is each source?
- 3.3 — What are the unit economics, in your goal's currency?
- 3.4 — Is there a credible path from here to where the idea needs to be?
- 3.5 — What is the likely competitive trajectory of this market, and where do you sit in it?
- 3.6 — If this does not work out, what would it cost across financial, time, energy, social, and mission dimensions?

## Layer IV — Fit
*Is this maker the one to do this work?*

- 4.1 — Do you have, or can you reasonably acquire in time, the skills the idea requires?
- 4.2 — Can you actually reach the people the idea is meant to serve, at a cost the unit economics can support?
- 4.3 — Can you stomach the schlep this idea requires?
- 4.4 — Does the rhythm of this work match the rhythm of your life?
- 4.5 — Will this work, on average, give you energy or take it from you?
- 4.6 — Is this an idea you could be proud of doing, regardless of outcome?

## Layer V — Defensibility / Durability
*What protects the work over time?*

**Defensibility path** *(winner-take-most contexts only)*
- 5.1 — Who will try to copy this, and how easily?
- 5.2 — What specifically protects you?
- 5.3 — Is the defensibility you have proportional to the threat?
- 5.4 — What erodes your position over time, even without competition?
- 5.5 — Could the idea become more defensible over time, by intent?

**Durability path** *(structurally fragmented or non-commercial contexts)*
- 5.6 — What allows this work to keep doing what it does well, for as long as it needs to do so?

## Layer VI — Integrity
*What does the work do, and what does it make the maker into?*

- 6.1 — Who benefits, who pays the cost, and are externalities defensible?
- 6.2 — Does the idea require any kind of deception to work?
- 6.3 — What would your most thoughtful critic say?
- 6.4 — Could the idea be misused, and how would you respond?
- 6.5 — Is the idea reversible if you discover, later, that you got it wrong?
- 6.6 — Is the daily texture of the work something you can stand behind?

---

*Eventually, you don't need this card. The questions become how you think about ideas. The plane becomes how you see them. At that point, the framework has done its work.*
